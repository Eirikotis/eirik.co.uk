## Credit protocol for Bittensor assets (project name: VOID)

This was a credit protocol for Bittensor built on Morpho isolated lending markets, with Bittensor subnet assets designed for use as collateral. The architecture combined isolated lending markets with cross-chain collateral handling, oracle and liquidation systems, risk-curated structures and market-specific underwriting. The venture was called VOID.

Eirik worked as Product & Commercial Lead. His responsibilities included:

- defining the product concept, architecture, requirements, user journeys and roadmap;
- translating the commercial opportunity into buildable product and system requirements;
- coordinating delivery through an eight-person development team and an external technical lead;
- setting scope, priorities, budget, deadlines and acceptance criteria;
- reviewing frontend, backend and smart-contract delivery without claiming to have personally written all production software;
- designing collateral eligibility, loan-to-value and debt-cap logic;
- analysing market capacity, AMM liquidity, price impact, flow volatility and stressed liquidation execution;
- considering oracle, bridge, router and liquidation dependencies;
- developing pricing, fee, revenue and commercial models;
- producing investor materials, lending documentation and technical-commercial explanations;
- leading investor meetings, product demonstrations, diligence and partner discussions.

The underlying opportunity was to create the underwriting, liquidity and operating standards needed for emerging network assets to support credit. The system reached functional implementation and detailed strategic discussions. It did not complete a fundraise, reach a scaled institutional launch or produce scaled TVL or revenue. The accurate conclusion is that substantial product, risk and venture work was completed, but the market timing and financing outcome did not support a scaled launch.

## Automated quantitative research for frontier markets (project name: Bittensor AutoResearch)

This was an automated quantitative and machine-learning research system Eirik built to search systematically for predictive signals and potential alpha in frontier Bittensor subnet markets, then test whether those signals survived realistic portfolio construction and unseen data. The project was called Bittensor AutoResearch.

The system included:

- canonical market datasets and repeatable refresh pipelines;
- cleaning, validation and subnet identity/lifecycle handling;
- 231 engineered features covering price, liquidity, flow, incentives and market structure;
- prediction targets over 1, 3, 7 and 14-day horizons;
- Ridge regression and XGBoost model families;
- cross-sectional ranking of subnet-market opportunities;
- walk-forward evaluation across hundreds of prediction origins;
- frozen holdouts and explicit information-leakage controls;
- model, feature-group and target comparisons;
- candidate ranking, confidence thresholds and position sizing;
- liquidity constraints, trading costs and AMM-based execution simulation;
- residual capital policy and portfolio simulation;
- reproducible experiment outputs and deterministic evaluation;
- bounded LLM-assisted workflows for proposing or implementing research improvements.

Eirik defined the research objective, designed the architecture and evaluation process, directed AI-assisted implementation, worked with Python and the data/model pipeline, compared research approaches, reviewed outputs and decided whether the evidence justified capital deployment.

Human judgement remained responsible for objectives, architecture and deployment decisions. The LLM assisted with bounded proposals and implementation; tests, metrics and simulations provided the evidence. The infrastructure worked, but the results did not justify claiming stable or profitable deployed alpha.
